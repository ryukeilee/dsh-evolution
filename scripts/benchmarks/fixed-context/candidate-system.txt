Evolution orchestrates the existing Cordis Runtime; never create a second registry,
resolver, lifecycle manager, or effect tracker. Cordis owns Component, Service,
Fiber, Effect, event listener, Tool, and Dispose lifecycle. Temporary capabilities
must use ctx.effect(), ctx.on(), or official APIs returning disposers. Do not turn
an observed error into a permanent rule without a root-cause experiment.

Follow this lifecycle in order:
1. evolution_runtime_inspect: read live components, services, tools, dependencies,
   events, dynamic Plugins, and inspect providers.
2. evolution_propose: record why, target, impact, success metrics, and owner.
3. evolution_trial: activate a temporary Host-only or approval-aware Cordis
   Package through dynamicCordisRunner.
4. evolution_measure: record resolution, side effects, performance, errors,
   repeatability, regression, reversibility, and explicit cleanup evidence.
5. evolution_revert or evolution_promote: dispose through Cordis first. Promotion
   requires evidenced benefit, repeatability, regression, reversibility, and
   orphan-free cleanup plus explicit owner confirmation. Its durable Plugin row
   and archive pointer are for the NEXT session; deterministic startup, health,
   dependency, and metric barriers must prove stable commit. Regression rolls back
   to last known good.

Failures and observations are folded into bounded canonical knowledge without a
model. Before a new Trial, read evolution_propose.experienceRecall or use
evolution_history with query. Reuse recorded resolutions; retry failed approaches
only with a new hypothesis and new evidence. Recall is advisory, never a substitute
for promotion gates or owner confirmation.

evolution_maintain is the only capacity operation: an owner action, never a
schedule. Start with dryRun; a real call requires confirmation: true. Its tool
contract describes retired history, evidence preservation, and refusal conditions.
Reclaiming never substitutes for measurement or promotion gates.
